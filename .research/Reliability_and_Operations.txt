1. Why Agent Reliability Is Different
Traditional SLOs rest on a deterministic contract: the same input produces the same output, and deviation is unambiguously a failure. Agentic systems break that contract — identical inputs can produce different but plausibly correct outputs, or quiet failures that never surface as errors.

The silent-failure problem
When an agent fails, the observability stack is frequently silent: HTTP 200, normal latency, zero error rate — while the agent approves something it should not have, writes to the wrong place, or loops through budget. Every mechanism in this document exists to make behavioural failure visible, because infrastructure monitoring alone will not surface it.

Consistency needs its own objective. Across 14 models over 18 months of releases, accuracy improved steadily while reliability improved only modestly. A model can get more answers right on average while becoming less predictable, and only a consistency SLI catches that.
2.1 Error budgets
	•	One budget per SLI, running in parallel. The accuracy budget can be healthy while the latency budget burns because a new model is slower.
	•	Budget health gates autonomy. A clean 30-day record earns a higher autonomy tier; a budget burning at multiples of the sustainable rate triggers escalation and demotion to a lower tier. This is what makes graduated autonomy operational rather than decorative.
	•	Budget exhaustion freezes deploys. Feature work stops, reliability work starts. The rule is written down in advance so it is not negotiated during the incident.

Loop-specific instrumentation. Agent loops break at the perceive or observe step rather than at reasoning; the dominant modes are runaway repetition, context degrading across iterations, and action taken on unverified output. Instrument all three, and enforce a hard iteration ceiling, a no-progress detector, and a per-run cost ceiling as runtime controls rather than dashboard metrics.

Containment precedes root cause. In the first five minutes the objective is blast-radius control, not diagnosis. If the incident followed a deploy, roll back unless there is hard evidence rollback will not help — and never combine rollback with opportunistic cleanup. Recover in measured tranches with a verification step between each; an over-confident first recovery is among the most common causes of a repeat incident.

Cache invalidation is part of rollback
A documented failure: a prompt edit passed a 1% canary on green dashboards, was promoted, and the semantic cache kept serving the bad answer for forty minutes after rollback. Rolling back the code does not roll back the cache. Cache and routing-memory invalidation are steps in the rollback runbook, not follow-up cleanup.

5. Regulatory Reporting
Serious-incident reporting under Article 73 runs on clocks measured in days, and the clock starts at awareness.

Trigger
Deadline
Serious incident (default)
15 days from awareness; immediately once causal link established
Widespread infringement / critical-infrastructure disruption
2 days
Death
10 days
Deployer notifies provider
"Immediately" — guidance reads as within 24 hours

The no-alteration rule conflicts with the instinct to fix
The provider must not perform any investigation that alters the AI system in a way which may affect subsequent evaluation before informing the competent authorities. That directly conflicts with hot-fixing, which is why Preserve is a distinct phase in Section 4. An incomplete initial report is expressly permitted to meet the deadline, so filing early with partial information is the correct move. Authorities may order measures within seven days, and non-compliance carries fines up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide turnover.

Pre-assign the reportability decision. A named role decides whether an incident is reportable, with a named deputy, and an internal deadline shorter than the regulatory one. This decision cannot be made for the first time during an incident with a two-day clock running.
6. Disaster Recovery
Tier the estate rather than applying one objective across it.

Analytics, historical eval scores

The workflow engine is the strong part. Workflow state writes are synchronously replicated across three availability zones before acknowledgement, giving zero RPO for an AZ failure, with multi-region replication targeting sub-one-minute RPO and automatic conflict resolution after failover.
Tier 3 is cheaper than it looks. Vector indexes are derived data, rebuildable from source documents, so their RPO is an ingestion-cost question rather than a data-loss question — provided the ingestion pipeline itself is Tier 2. Verify that dependency; if source documents live only in the index, the tiering is wrong.

DR breaks at the configuration layer
Most teams have the data side covered — backups run, RPO is tested. RTO collapses because IAM, networking and service settings are rebuilt manually under pressure mid-outage, and the gap between documented and actual infrastructure state widens further when agents make production changes. Capture configuration continuously so "last known good" is a real restore target, and include the configuration restore step in every DR test.
7. Human Oversight Operations

Time-boxed decision lanes matched to risk, failing safe to denied on timeout with partial context captured for audit. Cap review volume per reviewer per day, publish explicit rejection criteria, and close the feedback loop on overrides.
Instrument the rubber-stamp rate as a governance metric. A reviewer approving 99% of decisions at under ten seconds average review time is clicking, not reviewing. This belongs on the same dashboard as the SLOs, because degraded oversight is a reliability failure.

9. Definition of Done
	•	Two-week baseline measured; SLOs set from it and signed by engineering and product.
	•	Parallel error budgets live, with autonomy tiering wired to budget health.
	•	All seven detection panels deployed and alert-tested.
	•	Kill switch tested end-to-end, including mid-workflow.
	•	Rollback runbook complete per action class, including cache invalidation.
	•	Art.73 decision role assigned with deputy; evidence-preservation step rehearsed.
	•	DR test passed including configuration restore, not only data restore.
	•	Oversight lanes staffed; rubber-stamp metric instrumented.
